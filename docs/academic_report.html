1. Executive Summary & Problem Context

In contemporary criminal justice administration in Bangladesh, law enforcement stations (Thanas) and specialized investigation agencies face systemic challenges due to historical reliance on manual paper records, physical occurrence diaries, and disconnected filing cabinets. This conventional operational framework suffers from four critical vulnerabilities:

  1. Evidence Provenance & Admissibility Failures: Under Section 9 of the Evidence Act, physical and digital evidence seized at crime scenes must possess an unbroken, verifiable chronological custody record from field recovery to court exhibition. In paper systems, unrecorded transfers and lost custody logs routinely result in judicial dismissals on procedural grounds.
  2. Citizen Grievance Bottlenecks: Citizens filing complaints receive handwritten slips with no centralized tracking. Without a secure public tracking mechanism, citizens cannot track whether a complaint progressed to an inquiry, a General Diary (GD), or a First Information Report (FIR).
  3. Inter-Jurisdictional Information Silos: Organized criminal syndicates exploit jurisdictional boundaries across metropolitan commands (Dhaka, Chattogram, Sylhet, Rajshahi, Khulna). Disconnected station diaries prevent detectives from cross-referencing weapon ballistics, suspect biometrics, and modus operandi.
  4. Absence of Immutable Audit Accountability: Manual paper records lack access-control boundaries and mutation auditing, creating risks of unauthorized tampering.

ORCUS (Organized Crime Understanding System) resolves these challenges by implementing a Third Normal Form (3NF) relational database supporting the complete operational lifecycle of modern law enforcement: citizen intake, General Diary recording, statutory FIR registration under CrPC Section 154, multi-officer case management, and cryptographically verified evidence custody.

2. Relational Database Design & Normalization (3NF)

The database schema (orcus_db) comprises 28 normalized tables operating under MySQL 8.0+ / MariaDB 10.4+ InnoDB storage engine with strict foreign key constraints, guaranteeing transaction isolation and zero data redundancy.

2.1 Formal Normalization Proof

  • First Normal Form (1NF): All attributes contain atomic values. Multi-valued citizen contact channels (phone numbers, email addresses) are partitioned into an independent child relation complainant_contact. Primary keys are uniquely defined on every relation.
  • Second Normal Form (2NF): Satisfies 1NF. All non-prime attributes are fully functionally dependent on the entire primary key. In composite junction tables (case_suspect, case_victim, case_witness, fir_legal_section), operational attributes such as role_in_crime, impact_type, and testimony_summary depend strictly on the composite key pair.
  • Third Normal Form (3NF): Satisfies 2NF. No transitive dependencies exist ($X o Y o Z$ where $Z$ is non-prime). Geographic designations (division, district, thana) are isolated into reference entities. Officer credentials and branch postings reside across distinct entities (officer, user, agency_branch), preventing update anomalies when officer profile details change.

3.1 Enterprise Security & Token Secrecy Implementation

  • Zero Client-Side Token Exposure: Unlike standard SPAs that store JWTs in localStorage or sessionStorage (leaving tokens vulnerable to XSS exfiltration), ORCUS transmits JWTs exclusively inside secure HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax session cookies. Client-side scripts cannot access or read raw tokens.
  • Bcrypt Password Encryption: Passwords are never stored in plaintext; all credentials are encrypted using bcrypt.GenerateFromPassword(cost = 10).
  • Root Administrator Safeguard: The root administrator account (user_id = 1) is hard-locked at the API controller level. Any programmatic attempt to delete or demote user 1 throws an unbypassable 403 Forbidden response.
  • Transactional Deletion Cascade: When an operational entity is deleted by an authorized administrator, the database and backend execute transactional cascades across junction records while recording a detailed entry in audit_log.

4. End-to-End Operational Workflows ("How What Works")

4.1 Workflow Stage 1: Public Citizen Complaint to Statutory FIR

When a citizen reports an incident via the public portal or police desk:

  1. The complainant submits incident title, description, category, and contact details.
  2. The system executes an atomic increment on system_sequence for key CMP-2026, generating a collision-free tracking code (e.g., CMP-2026-0801).
  3. The record is saved with status Submitted and assigned to the receiving command branch.
  4. The citizen tracks status online using the tracking code. Public inquiries display sanitized updates (public_status_message) while protecting confidential police investigations (internal_notes).
  5. A reviewing Duty Officer inspects the complaint. If criminal elements are substantiated under CrPC Section 154, the officer elevates the complaint to an official First Information Report (FIR).
  6. The system atomically issues an FIR number (FIR-2026-0101) and maps statutory Penal Code sections (e.g., Penal Code 420 for Fraud, 120B for Conspiracy).

4.2 Workflow Stage 2: Case Dossier & Detective Assignment

Following FIR registration, the Officer-in-Charge opens an official investigation file (CAS-2026-XXXX) and delegates an elite detective as Lead Investigating Officer (IO).

  • Assignment Tracking: Recorded into case_assignment_history with handover remarks, branch assignment, and effective timestamps.
  • Status Lifecycle: Transitions through strictly defined milestones:
    Open → Under Investigation → Pending Review → Closed ↔ Reopened. Every transition requires operational justification and is logged into case_status_history.

4.3 Workflow Stage 3: Participants Profiling & GIS Mapping

  • Suspect Ingestion: Suspects are cataloged with physical identification marks, biometrics, suspicion levels, and custody statuses (Under Surveillance, Arrested, Interrogated, Wanted).
  • Relational Bridges: M:N bridge tables (case_suspect, case_victim, case_witness) link participants to multiple investigations, defining specific operational roles such as Principal Hacker, Account Mule, or Extortion Caller.
  • Witness Protection: Witnesses receive reliability scores and state protection flags; signed testimonies are archived in the case file.

4.4 Workflow Stage 4: Tamper-Proof Evidence & Chain of Custody

Under Section 9 of the Evidence Act, evidence must possess an unbroken record of possession. ORCUS enforces this through a 4-step custody lifecycle:

  1. Seizure & Packaging: The field officer secures the item in an anti-static or tamper-evident pouch and applies a numbered barcode seal (SEAL-DHK-0091).
  2. Cryptographic Digital Hashing: Digital media (hard drives, flash media, VoIP packet captures) receive an immediate SHA-256 checksum (e.g. e3b0c44298fc1c14...) stored in the database. If a single byte is modified during analysis, the hash verification fails.
  3. Multi-Hop Transfer Logging: Any movement between facilities (Scene → Forensics Lab Locker → Ballistics Vault → Courtroom) generates a mandatory transaction in evidence_status_history specifying releasing custodian, receiving custodian, seal condition (Intact, Broken, Resealed), and operational reason.
  4. Court Exhibition: The item is admitted as a physical court exhibit (e.g. Exhibit P-1), completing an unbroken audit trail for trial judges.
